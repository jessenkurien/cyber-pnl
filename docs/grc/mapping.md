# GRC Mapping and Uses

Cyber P&L **maps to** and **supports** the following. It does not make anyone compliant; it gives the risk function a quantified, governed input that these frameworks ask for.

## Frameworks

| Framework | Where this fits |
|---|---|
| **NIST CSF 2.0** | GV.RM (risk strategy and appetite), GV.OC (business context), ID.RA (scenario assessment and prioritization), and GV.OV (review and oversight). This is an implementation mapping, not a NIST validation. |
| **ISO/IEC 27005:2022** | Risk identification (scenarios), analysis (frequency × magnitude with ranges), evaluation (appetite comparison), treatment (investment ranking), monitoring (KPI-driven refresh) |
| **ISO/IEC 27001:2022** | Risk-assessment criteria and treatment planning. The project does not provide certification or enough evidence to establish conformity. |
| **COSO ERM / ISO 31000** | Cyber risk expressed in the same units as other enterprise risks, so it can sit in the enterprise risk register without translation |
| **NIST SP 800-30** | The scenario/likelihood/impact structure, with quantitative rather than ordinal scales |
| **SOC 2** | CC3.2 (risk identification and analysis), CC9.1 (risk mitigation decisions with rationale) |

## Regulatory and disclosure uses

**SEC cybersecurity disclosure (Item 106 of Regulation S-K).** The rule requires registrants to
describe processes for assessing, identifying, and managing material risks from cybersecurity threats,
as well as board oversight and management's role. A reviewed model may support such a process, but the
existence of Cyber P&L does not establish compliance, disclosure adequacy, or materiality. Preserve
authenticated approval and source evidence outside the repository when needed. See the
[SEC compliance guide](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/small-business-compliance-guides/cybersecurity-risk-management-strategy-governance-incident-disclosure).

**EU NIS2 / DORA.** NIS2 imposes cybersecurity risk-management and management-body duties on entities
within its scope, subject to national implementation. DORA imposes ICT-risk governance and documented
risk-management duties on covered financial entities. A quantified statement may support internal
reasoning; it is not direct proof of compliance or management approval. Review applicability with
counsel. See the official [NIS2 text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2022/2555/oj/eng) and
[DORA text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj/eng).

**GDPR Article 32.** A scenario model can be one input to risk-informed security decisions, but dollar
quantification alone does not satisfy Article 32. The controller or processor still needs the required
technical, organizational, and evidentiary work.

## Cyber insurance

The loss exceedance curve can support retention and limit discussions with brokers, insurers, and
Finance. It does not determine the right retention or substitute for an insurer's model. Present the
curve as management's decision-support estimate and provide underlying evidence separately.

## Board and committee reporting

Quarterly: expected loss, bad-year figure, appetite status, top three drivers, top three assumptions, changes since last quarter and why (KPI moved, incident recalibrated, new control). Annually: methodology review, calibration log, control value table, investment outcomes versus their pre-funding estimates.

## Claims you may and may not make

May: "estimate," "input interval," "supports," "maps to," and "documented risk-management input."
May not: "predicts," "compliant," "certified," "actuarially validated" (unless an actuary performed
that work), "digitally signed" (unless it is), or "confidence interval" for an annual-loss percentile.
